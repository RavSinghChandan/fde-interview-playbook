# Your FDE positioning

## One-line version

I am a software engineer who has moved from dependable backend systems into applied AI, and I enjoy staying responsible for the whole path: understanding the user's problem, building the smallest useful solution, integrating it into real workflows, and improving it from evidence.

## Why this is credible for you

- **Systems foundation:** Banking and airline systems taught you to respect existing services, data flows, operational constraints, and failure recovery.
- **Applied AI ownership:** Poultry 360, Aaina, and Aura with Rav show that you can turn a real need into a deployed product rather than stop at a notebook or demo.
- **Reliability mindset:** You have examples of evaluation catching routing errors, preserving user dislikes, guarding against invented numbers, adding security checks, and putting human approval in a flow.
- **Customer context:** Poultry 360's low-literacy and multilingual design is evidence that the interface and operating context matter as much as the model.
- **Learning in public:** 106 merged PRs show that you can work inside unfamiliar codebases, respond to review, and improve software used by others.

## The transition story

“I started with backend and distributed systems, where correctness and integration matter because other systems and people depend on them. As I moved into AI, I kept the same production mindset. I built systems around actual workflows, added evaluations and guardrails when I found failure cases, and shipped the products for real users. That combination of customer problem solving and engineering through deployment is why I am targeting Forward Deployed Engineering.”

## Honest boundaries

- Say **“I built”** only for work you personally did; name collaborators and shared ownership where relevant.
- For enterprise platform work, explain the responsibility you personally held without sharing confidential customer or company information.
- For each metric, be prepared to say what was measured, over what period, and how it changed.
- Your CV calls out 4+ years in its summary and lists roles from 2022. Use a consistent, current experience calculation in interviews; avoid switching between “4+” and “5 years” without explaining the dates.
